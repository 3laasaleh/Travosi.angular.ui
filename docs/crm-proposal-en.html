1. The commercial pipeline

Customer  →  Quotation  →  Booking  →  Invoice  →  Voucher

Every stage lives in the same system, on the same customer record, priced in the same currency, and every commercial document can be exported as a professional PDF and sent to the traveller. Your agents stop working in Excel and WhatsApp, and management finally sees the whole pipeline in one place.

The CRM is a secure, role-controlled area behind login, available in English and Arabic with full right-to-left layout, and excluded from search engines.

2. Quotations

The heart of the sales process: build a complete travel offer in one screen and send it as a PDF.

2.1 Building a quotation

  • Select the customer, the currency (EGP or USD), the travel start and end dates, and the number of adults, children and infants.
  • Pull items straight from your catalogue: packages, tours, hotels and flights — multi-select, no re-typing.
  • Add any number of transfers: from, to, pick-up time and arrival time.
  • Add any number of policies and terms lines (cancellation, payment, baggage, visa, whatever you need).
  • Apply a discount and a tax rate; the system calculates subtotal, discount, tax, grand total and internal cost automatically as you type.
  • Set a validity date — the offer visibly expires, so agents stop honouring stale prices.
  • Free-text notes for anything specific to the trip.

2.2 Lifecycle and controls

StatusMeaning and allowed next steps
DraftEditable. Can be sent or cancelled. Only drafts can be edited or deleted — no silent changes after a customer has seen the price.
SentIssued to the customer. Can be marked Accepted, Rejected, Expired or Cancelled.
Accepted / Rejected / Expired / CancelledFinal states, kept for history and reporting.

2.3 Actions

  • Download PDF — a branded, print-ready quotation document.
  • Duplicate — clone an existing quotation for a similar trip in one click; the fastest way to quote a repeat itinerary.
  • Guided status changes: the system only offers the transitions that are actually valid.
  • Safe deletion, restricted to drafts and cancelled offers.

2.4 Built-in validation

  • Travel end date must follow the start date.
  • Validity date must be in the future and cannot be after the travel start.
  • Traveller counts must be whole numbers; a discount can never exceed the subtotal.
  • Transfer pick-up must be in the future and arrival must be after pick-up.

3. Invoices

  • Create an invoice against a customer with invoice date, due date and currency.
  • Line items pulled from the catalogue — packages, tours, hotels, flights — plus free-form transfer lines with route, date, pick-up and arrival times.
  • Per-line quantity, unit price, line discount and line total.
  • Invoice-level discount and tax rate; subtotal, discount, tax and total are calculated live, with the discount capped at the subtotal.
  • Automatic invoice numbering, notes field, and full edit history.
  • Download PDF — the exported file is named after the invoice number, ready to e-mail or file.
  • Protected deletion with confirmation.

4. Travel vouchers

The document the traveller actually carries. Five voucher types are supported:

Voucher typeWhat it confirms
HotelProperty, service dates, check-in / check-out.
FlightFlight record, departure and arrival details.
TourThe booked tour and its date.
PackageThe booked package and its date range.
TransferFrom, to, pick-up time and arrival time.
  • Each voucher is tied to a customer and to a real catalogue record — no free typing of hotel names.
  • Automatic voucher numbering and PDF export named after the voucher number.
  • Validation of the service date range, mandatory service selection, and future-dated transfer schedules with arrival after pick-up.

5. Bookings desk

  • Central queue of every booking request coming from the website, paginated and filterable by status.
  • Status workflow: Pending → Confirmed → Completed, or Cancelled at any point.
  • Confirm a booking with an optional phone-confirmation note recorded against it.
  • Cancel with either free cancellation or a recorded cancellation fee.
  • Complete a booking after travel — which is also what unlocks the customer's right to leave a review on the website.
  • Assign an agent to each booking, so ownership and commission are never ambiguous.

6. Customer database

  • Customer types: Individual, Couple, Family, Company — company name becomes mandatory for corporate accounts.
  • Full record: first and last name, e-mail, mobile, passport number, date of birth, gender, type, assigned agent and active status.
  • Companion travellers attached to the customer: spouse, child, infant, adult or babysitter — so a family quotation is built once.
  • Activate / deactivate customers; deletion is only possible for inactive records, protecting your history.
  • Every customer is owned by an agent.

7. Operational modules

7.1 Hotels

  • Name, star rating (1–5), destination, address, description, phone, e-mail, website, active flag.
  • Feeds quotations, invoices and hotel vouchers.

7.2 Airlines

  • Name, IATA/ICAO code and logo.
  • Professional image pipeline: file-type and 5 MB size checks, automatic resizing and conversion to WebP before upload — logos stay sharp and the system stays fast.

7.3 Flights

  • Flight number, airline, departure and arrival airports, times, price, available seats and class (Economy, Business, First).
  • Airport search with autocomplete and keyboard navigation.
  • Validation prevents identical departure/arrival airports or an arrival before departure.

7.4 Task management

  • Task types matched to travel operations: follow up a customer, prepare a quotation, confirm a booking, collect a payment, request documents, customer support, general.
  • Priorities: Low, Medium, High, Urgent. Lifecycle: Pending → In progress → Completed → Closed, or Returned with a reason.
  • Admins create and assign; agents start, finish (with a completion note), return or close their own tasks.
  • In-app notifications in the top bar keep agents on top of their queue.

7.5 Contact-message inbox

  • Every website contact form submission lands here: name, phone, e-mail, question and timestamp.
  • All / unread / read tabs, mark-as-read and delete — nothing gets lost in a shared mailbox.

7.6 Catalogue management (shared with the website)

  • Destinations, cities, tours, packages and blog articles — bilingual authoring, image management and publication status.
  • Discount campaigns on tours and packages: date-bound percentage discounts, stop and restart, a full history with Active / Scheduled / Stopped / Expired badges, and a one-click notify customers action that queues promotional e-mails.

8. Security, roles and quality

  • Three roles: Admin, Agent, Customer.
  • Every CRM route protected by authentication and role guards.
  • Agents see their own tasks; admins see everything.
  • JWT tokens with automatic session handling.
  • Confirmation dialogs before every destructive action.
  • Field-level validation with clear messages in both languages.
  • Server-side pagination (10 / 20 / 50) on every list.
  • Excluded from search engines by design.
